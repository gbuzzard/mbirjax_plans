## 1. The ground truth the table is measured against

**`_apply_device_policy` is at `mbirtorch/tomography_model.py:890`, and it has
exactly ONE call site in the whole package: `tomography_model.py:2574`, inside
`vcd_recon`.**  Verified by grep across `mbirtorch/`.

Two consequences that shape every row below:

* **`torch.cuda.device_count()` appears in only four places package-wide**:
  `tomography_model.py:934` (the policy), `tomography_model.py:839` and `:843`
  (validation inside `configure_devices`, where the caller named the count),
  and `memory_stats.py:50` (reporting).  On today's `greg_dev` there is **no
  second device-choosing site anywhere.**  One arrives with the prerelease
  merge — see §3.
* **The placements default to trivial single-device**
  (`tomography_model.py:221`, `:232`: `Placement([self.torch_device], ...)`).
  So an entry that "inherits the current placement" is *single-device on a
  fresh model* and becomes multi-device only after `configure_devices`
  (`:845`) or after a prior `recon`/`prox_map` widened it (`_settle` →
  `_install_device_layout`, `:1114`).

The env pin `MBIRTORCH_NUM_DEVICES` is read in exactly one function,
`_memory_ledger.pinned_device_count()` (`_memory_ledger.py:981`), called only
from `_apply_device_policy` (`tomography_model.py:933`).  **Any entry that does
not take the policy call also does not honor the process-wide pin** — including
the suite pin `tests/conftest.py:72`.

The ledger the policy prices (`_memory_ledger.plan_from_model`,
`_memory_ledger.py:738`) is a **full VCD-recon plan**: granularity, partition
sequence, prior/Hessian terms, view-batch charge.  The widening floors are
measured by `dev_scripts/refresh_widening_floors.py:284`
(`model.recon(..., max_iterations=ITERATIONS)`) — i.e. **calibrated on
3-iteration VCD recons, not on FBP/FDK, not on preprocessing, not on the
denoiser sweep.**  Both facts matter for column 5 and drive design question D4.

Legend for column 4:
**POLICY** = reaches `_apply_device_policy`; **INHERIT** = uses
`self.sino_placement`/`recon_placement` or a placement read off an argument;
**OWN** = chooses devices by its own rule; **PIN** = calls `configure_devices`
itself; **HOST** = no device allocation.

## 4. Design questions for a human ruling

**D1. Does an entry that runs before any model exists consult a policy at all — and if not, must it still honor the pins?**
`scan_to_sino` and the four `get_sino_and_model` readers do their device work *before* a `TomographyModel` exists, so there is no `sinogram_shape` parameter, no ledger plan, no `configure_devices` flag, and no `_floor_family` to consult.  The prerelease commit resolved this by giving preprocessing its own rule (all visible CUDA devices); the uniformity ruling says entries should take the same policy call.  Three coherent answers: (a) build a **model-free policy entry** that takes a shape and a workload kind, and route both preprocessing and the recon entries through it; (b) declare preprocessing **exempt but pin-obedient** — it may use all visible devices, but `MBIRTORCH_NUM_DEVICES` and a `devices=` argument must still cap it; (c) require preprocessing to be handed a model.  Today's merged behavior is none of these: it is exempt *and* pin-deaf, which will silently break the suite pin and any batch-queue pin.

**D2. When an entry inherits a layout from a previous recon on the same model, may it re-choose?**
The policy is re-evaluated at every automatic entry by design (`tomography_model.py:903-908`).  Adding policy calls to `fbp_recon`/`fdk_recon`/`recon_plastic_metal` multiplies the number of times a count can change on one model.  A count change reinstalls the placements (`:1114`), and every `Shards` a caller still holds — from `prepare_sino_for_devices` (A9), a precomputed `fm_hessian` (A8), an `output_sharded=True` recon, or a BH pass's `init_recon` (D7) — becomes unusable with "belong to a different device configuration" (`:1154`).  Rule needed: may a **non-recon** entry only *set* a layout that is still at its default, and otherwise inherit?  Or may every entry re-litigate, with the invalidation documented as the price?  Note the mitigation that already exists: `_settle` reinstalls **only** when the count actually changes, so identical inputs are free.

**D3. Should `QGGMRFDenoiser.denoise` take the policy call now that its loop can run sharded?**
The prerelease port removes the technical objection but keeps the denoiser outside the policy by construction.  If it comes inside, two things break their meaning: `_floor_family` is `None` → the **parallel-beam** floors govern a workload with no projector at all, and `sinogram_elements` would be counting **image voxels** because the denoiser sets `sinogram_shape == image_shape` (`denoising.py:109`).  The ledger plan is worse: it prices view batches, projector transients, and a partition sequence the denoiser does not have.  Either the denoiser gets its own floor family and ledger shape, or it stays explicitly exempt like the autograd wrappers (F6) — but the stale reason in `_apply_device_policy`'s docstring must be corrected either way.

**D4. Do the recon-calibrated floors and the full-recon ledger apply to workloads that are not VCD recons?**
The floors come from `model.recon(..., 3 iterations)` (`dev_scripts/refresh_widening_floors.py:284`); the ledger is a full VCD plan (`_memory_ledger.py:738`).  Applying both unchanged to `fbp_recon`/`fdk_recon` means (i) the memory estimate charges prior, Hessian, and partition terms that a direct recon never allocates — over-pricing, so FBP would be held at fewer devices than it can actually use; and (ii) a crossover measured on an iterative loop decides a single filtered backprojection, whose scaling with device count is a different curve.  Cone is the sharp case: `_floor_family='cone'` never admits n=2, so `fdk_recon` would be pinned to 1 or 4 by a rule measured on something else.  Uniformity as literally stated ("the same floors, the same full-recon ledger plan") is implementable and consistent, but it is knowingly wrong in magnitude for non-recon entries — worth confirming that consistency is the intended trade.

**D5 (smaller, but needs a decision).** Two existing sites decide a device set outside the policy without searching a count: `generate_demo_data`'s `devices=` argument (E1) and the vcls single-view sibling's hard pin to the parent's lead device (G1).  Both route through `configure_devices`, so they are legitimate today, but under a uniform policy they are the two places where "the library chose" and "the caller chose" are indistinguishable in the run log.  Decide whether they keep their pins with a recorded reason (the F6 pattern) or become policy consumers.
