Does the per-granularity schedule remove the streaks? The Phase B evaluation

The sharpness/snr_db schedule study, Phase B (2026-07-25/26), branch sharpness_schedule. Plan of record: phase_b_plan.md (pre-registered criteria) · Phase A background: findings.html (the artifact, the metrics, and their validation) · scripts: plans/experiments/sharpness_schedule/schedule/.

TL;DR

Phase B asked whether the per-granularity schedule reduces the partition-driven streak severity without harming convergence. The schedule runs conservative regularization on the coarse early subsets and target values on the fine ones. The pre-registered answer is no.

  • All six variants failed C1, the streak criterion, and they failed in the harmful direction. At iteration 14 the two-seed Slow is 4–22% worse than baseline. C1 required ≤50% of baseline. The injection peaks are 11–57% worse. These are real effects against a measured 1% pair noise. They are visible from iteration 0 onward.
  • C2, the no-harm criterion, passed everywhere. The target-objective gaps are +0.02–0.04% against a 0.5% allowance. These gaps indicate that the schedules cost almost nothing. The schedules simply make the artifact worse rather than better.
  • The mechanism reading is clean. Prior-side schedules are consistently worse than data-side schedules at matched depth. That ordering is the pre-registered signature of the saturation mechanism. Freshly injected differences are pushed into the prior's saturated regime while the artifact forms.

Decision (per the pre-registered rules): there is no winner. The b = 8 depth extension was not run. That extension was reserved for a flat response, and the measured response is clearly directional. There is no full-resolution confirmation. The search stopped for discussion. §6 lays out what the result redirects.

1. What was tested, and how it is judged

Phase A established that the streak artifact is injected by the coarse early subsets. Phase A also measured the artifact with the two-seed Slow. That measurement runs two reconstructions differing only in their partition seed. Slow is the low-axial-frequency energy of the fine-scale error in their difference. The Phase A page carries the definitions and their validation.

Phase B evaluates the remedy, one (σx, σy) pair per granularity. The coarse early iterations run at conservative regularization, and the fine iterations run at the target values. The objective is convex, so the converged answer is untouched and only the path changes.

A schedule variant is a family and a depth. The depth b is the amount of regularization added per granularity level. It is measured in decibels of the data-to-prior balance σx²/σy². In those units, sharpness +1 ≈ +6.02 dB. The level d counts granularities below the finest. For the default sequence's 4-, 16- and 64-subset iterations, d = 3, 2, 1. The three families apply the same balance decrement through different knobs:

Each family was run at two depths, b = 2 and b = 4 dB per level. A variant is named by its family letter and depth: D2, D4, S2, S4, J2, J4. Those six variants are judged against the unscheduled baseline.

The judgment is pre-registered (phase_b_plan.md). Its thresholds were frozen after a noise-calibration step (B0) on the existing Phase A runs. That step put the seed-pair spread of the final Slow at 1% (downsampled) and 4% (full resolution). It put the es_rmse seed spread at ≤1.5×10−4. The criteria below set their thresholds far above these spreads. The judgment has three criteria:

  • C1 (streak benefit): two-seed Slow at iteration 14 must be ≤ 50% of baseline at both the downsampled and full-resolution scales, for every seed pair. The peak over the snapshot grid must also be ≤ 70% of baseline's peak. That second requirement asks a variant to reduce injection, not merely to speed the heal.
  • C2 (no harm): the objective evaluated at the TARGET parameters must be within +0.5% of baseline at iteration 14. That objective is the downsampled arbiter. At full resolution the arbiters are es_rmse and the data term. The es_rmse arbiter is the root-mean-square sinogram residual, and it is independent of the sigma parameters. C2 also allows a bounded fallback at iteration 16 against baseline's iteration 14. All runs are 17 iterations, numbered 0–16, with early stopping disabled.
  • C3 (no harm where nothing is wrong): the same ≤+0.5% applies on the synthetic control. That control has no artifact.

Metrics are not the whole judgment. Every run keeps permanent image panels: the final reconstruction and error, and per-iteration error views. §2 shows the key panels inline. Any promotion decision would also rest on a visual review of the full set.

2. The search: six variants on the downsampled scan

The search ran the six variants and the baseline on the downsampled BGA scan. The run conditions were seeds {1, 2}, 17 iterations, and snapshots on the same iteration grid as Phase A ({0–5, 9, 14, 16}). Every run went through the same segmented driver as every Phase A run.

The driver's equivalence gate passed on the GPU first. That gate included a new identity check, which confirmed that a schedule with all offsets zero reproduces the unscheduled baseline. Two further identity checks confirmed the design intent. The automatically derived target (σx, σy) values are bitwise-identical across all runs, and every scheduled run uses exactly those values on its finest-granularity iterations. Before interpreting, one rival reading must be closed. Could the elevated Slow at a fixed iteration simply be a convergence-lag effect, with any slower variant scoring worse at equal iteration count? The recorded diagnostics refute this decisively. On both global progress rulers, the variants are barely behind at all. Their objective and es_rmse gaps of +0.02–0.04% correspond to 0.04–0.09 iterations of baseline progress. On the severity trajectory, their elevation corresponds to 0.4–1.8 iterations, a 5–40× mismatch. One case is sharper still. At iteration 0 the D variants are actually ahead of baseline on the residual, with es_rmse at −0.15% and −0.27%. At that same iteration they sit +37% and +20% on severity. These results indicate that the elevation is specific to the partition-driven artifact, not a by-product of slower convergence.

The most informative detail is then where the elevation appears. It appears immediately. At iteration 0 the variants already sit 20–76% above baseline, before any fine iteration has run. The values are D4 +20%, D2 +37%, and S4 +76%. That timing refutes the deferral story this study half-expected, in which injection is pushed into the fine phase.

What remains is a family of closely related readings that the present data do not separate. On the first reading, the coarse phase's strong steps erase seed-dependent error by homogenizing the residual across subsets, so throttling them slows the erasure. On the second, each conservative early subset leaves a larger residual for the later coarse subsets, which then inject more. That second reading is the Phase A footprint mechanism run in reverse. On either reading, the marginal effect of stronger coarse steps is net erasure at the downsampled scale. One caveat is recorded against both readings. The elevation is non-monotone in depth in the D family (§5), so the story is incomplete.

One diagnostic did move as the hypothesis predicted. That move comes with a catch. The footprint enrichment E(0) softened in every variant, from 1.41 to 1.33–1.37. The first-updated subset's share of the streak energy therefore fell. The map's total energy rose by a similar factor, however. The absolute streak energy on the first-updated subset's cylinders was therefore essentially unchanged, moving −3% to +1%. These numbers indicate that the schedule redistributed the association without reducing the injection it was aimed at.

3. Why there is no full-resolution confirmation

The plan's decision rules promote a variant to full-resolution confirmation only after it passes C1 + C2 on the downsampled search. No variant did, so the confirmation was not run. That decision was made by rule, not by judgment, and it is worth stating precisely. The full-resolution question is closed by pre-registration, not by direct measurement.

The downsampled result still argues on observational grounds that a full-resolution benefit is unlikely. Every variant's injection peak worsened, by +11–57%. Phase A measured recovery from the peak at both scales. Full resolution recovers far more slowly: a factor of 3.6 from the peak, against 9 downsampled. Elevated peaks should therefore persist harder at full resolution, not resolve.

A deliberate exploratory full-resolution check of D4, the least-harmful variant, remains an option for discussion. Such a check would be clearly labeled as outside the pre-registered protocol.

4. The guards that did not fire

The remaining protocol elements were conditioned on outcomes that did not occur, and they correctly never ran. Those elements were the third seeds, the 40-iteration merge demonstration, the synthetic no-harm runs (C3), and the b = 8 depth extension. The third seeds were reserved for near-threshold calls, and nothing was near threshold. The failures exceed the 1% pair noise by factors of 4–22. The merge demonstration and the synthetic no-harm runs were both conditioned on a winner. The b = 8 depth extension was reserved for a flat C1 response, and the response was directional.

The registered run-health diagnostics were recorded and are clean. No line-search clips occurred at either bound. The preconditioner prior-share behaved as designed, rising with schedule depth in the coarse phase. §5 uses that prior-share.

The B0 noise calibration stands as this page's measurement-noise reference: a final-Slow pair spread of 1% (downsampled) and 4% (full resolution), and an es_rmse seed spread of ≤1.5×10−4.

5. The mechanism reading: which knob mattered?

The families were built so that, at equal depth b, the D and S coarse-phase sigma pairs differ by an exact common multiplier. Every update term except the prior's saturation-threshold factors is invariant under that scaling. Any systematic D–S difference is therefore a threshold effect. The plan registered readings for both possible directions. The direction below is the one in which the prior-side family performs worse.

The data give a clear sign. S is consistently worse than D at equal depth. At iteration 14 the S and D severities are 0.00176 vs 0.00168 at b = 2, and 0.00184 vs 0.00158 at b = 4. At iteration 0 the b = 4 severities are 0.0128 vs 0.0087.

Per the registered interpretation, this ordering is the signature of saturation during formation. The S family lowers the early edge threshold T·σx. That threshold is the difference magnitude at which the qGGMRF prior treats a voxel-neighbor difference as an edge. Beyond that magnitude the prior's restoring force saturates. Lowering the threshold pushes freshly injected differences into the saturated regime, where the prior is weakest. The schedule freezes the very structure it was meant to prevent.

Two quantitative checks corroborate the threshold reading. The first check is the depth trend. The D–S difference grows with depth, and the S/D severity ratio at iteration 14 rises from 1.05 at b = 2 to 1.17 at b = 4. A threshold effect should behave that way when the threshold falls further. The second check is the J family, which carries half of each knob by construction. J lands almost exactly halfway between D and S on a log scale, at an interpolation fraction of 0.54 at both depths. That result indicates the response is smooth in the designed coordinates, not idiosyncratic per family.

Two observations are recorded without over-interpretation. The first is that the D family is non-monotone in depth. D4 is less harmful than D2 at every iteration, including iteration 0 (+20% vs +37%). That is a formation-phase property that the simple slowed-erasure story does not predict. The natural variable for a future investigation is the recorded preconditioner prior-share, which shifts regime across depth. At iteration 0 the prior-share is 0.35 for baseline, 0.69 for D2, and 0.90 for D4. At 0.90 the update is prior-dominated.

The second observation is that the E(0) result (§2) is a redistribution, not a reduction. The first-updated subset's share fell while its absolute streak energy stayed flat. These numbers indicate that the first-subset bias is real but is not the controlling term in total severity at the downsampled scale.

6. Where this leaves the remedy question

The per-granularity regularization schedule is not the remedy. This verdict covers the schedule as constructed here, not every possible schedule. On the downsampled scan it fails C1 in the harmful direction at every family and depth. It costs essentially nothing in convergence. This is a clean, pre-registered negative, and it earns two redirections:

  • The target moves from “early oversteps” to “subset homogenization”. Subset homogenization is the erasure mechanism §2 identified. The artifact's severity tracks how quickly the iteration erases seed-dependent error. The marginal effect of stronger coarse steps is net erasure at the downsampled scale (§2). The coarse phase still injects the artifact. Weakening it removes more erasure than injection. Remedies that act on the seed-dependent field directly look better aimed than schedules that throttle the early iterations. Two such remedies are at hand: the per-slice damping family already in the library, and mode-selective extensions of it. The damping family demonstrably suppresses the z-varying component on the real scan.
  • The saturation finding gives a second lever. The S-family's failure mode says the prior's threshold should stay HIGH while streaks form. Any future schedule attempt should therefore not lower T·σx early. Among everything tested, the baseline was best. The opposite direction, an aggressive-early schedule, was not tested, since only conservative decrements were run. The data therefore neither support nor rule out that direction. Caution rather than data argues against it. Phase A showed globally aggressive settings worsen severity. The D-family's depth non-monotonicity also warns against extrapolating smooth trends through b = 0.

Two discussion items go to the technical lead: whether to run the D4 check (§3), and whether Phase C pivots to the damping-family direction. The D4 check would sit outside the protocol. It would cheaply bound whether the schedule helps where the artifact persists.

One caution applies to the testbeds for that pivot. The hardened synthetic (Phase A page, Appendix B) is a fast testbed for truncation-driven streaking. As built, it is damping-insensitive. A damping-family remedy iterated there would read as a null regardless of its real-scan value. Damping-family work should therefore be evaluated on the downsampled real scan. The alternative is to extend the testbed first, until it shows real-scan-like damping sensitivity.

A visual pass over the per-run image panels supports both discussion items, the D4 check and the pivot choice. Those panels are kept for every run, and Provenance gives their locations.
