The second family: beam hardening, its instrument, and its testbed

The sharpness/snr_db schedule study, beam-hardening arc (2026-07-26), branch sharpness_schedule. Background: converged_streaks.html (the deposit mechanism and the padding results this page follows from) · scripts: plans/experiments/sharpness_schedule/{real_bga, mechanism, driver}/ · demo: demo/demo_10_mar.py.

TL;DR

Lateral padding removed the truncation streaks from the real scan. It also unmasked a second artifact family: near-horizontal, ray-aligned bands around the ball grid. The working hypothesis for that family is beam hardening. That hypothesis is now instrumented, tested, and reproduced on the synthetic testbed.

  • The instrument is the residual transfer curve. Bin the converged run's residual sinogram by the MAR-segmented metal coordinate. That coordinate comes from segmenting the reconstruction and projecting the metal. On the real padded scan the residual dips on metal-heavy rays. That downward direction is the hardening sign. The unpadded scan shows no metal organization. Truncation swamps it. The plastic-coordinate control is flat.
  • A two-material polychromatic testbed reproduces the hardening family. With hardening as the only pathology, the reconstruction develops three features: cupping, interball dark bands, and z-localized face bands. A rotated-view-set control gives an error correlation of 0.982. That result proves the structure is object-anchored, not view aliasing.
  • The deposit framework generalizes with sign. Truncation deposits +32% of true mass. Hardening at full severity removes mass, −17% on the same ledger. Unmodeled mass of either sign is deposited at the expression scale the prior allows.
  • The two families are instrumentally orthogonal. The vertical-family severity score Slow stays at its floor across every hardened case. These results indicate that each family needs its own instrument. Each family now has one.

Product. This arc delivers three things: a severity dial s calibrated against the real scan, a ground-truth-equipped testbed for the metal-artifact-reduction work on the release goals list, and a user-facing demo (demo_10_mar.py).

In this parametrization the real padded residual dip sits near s ≈ 0.2.

1. The question the padding results left open

When the real scan is reconstructed with lateral padding, the truncation family largely disappears. That family is the fine vertical, z-coherent striping this study began with. What remains visible is different in kind: bands roughly aligned with the source-to-detector rays, stamped at the heights of the ball rows, plus fine texture at the package faces.

In the (x, z) view, cone rays are near-horizontal lines. Per-ray data errors therefore backproject as near-horizontal bands. The geometry of the residual family itself says the error is being generated per ray, in the data domain. The natural cause for per-ray error on metal-heavy paths is beam hardening. This scan is uncorrected (bga_no_hart).

But "looks ray-aligned" is not an attribution. Two things were needed: an instrument that measures ray-association without assuming the answer, and a testbed where hardening can be switched on alone.

2. The instrument: residual transfer curves in the metal coordinate

If the error is generated per ray, measure it where that statement is native: the sinogram domain. At (near-)convergence the residual e = y − Âx is exactly what the linear model refused to explain. For each ray we also have a physical cause coordinate: the metal thickness it traverses. Call that thickness the metal coordinate m. The value of m is obtained exactly as the library's MAR machinery obtains it, in mbirjax.preprocess. The steps are Otsu segmentation of the reconstruction, then forward projection of the metal class. The transfer curve therefore lives in the same coordinate the MAR beam-hardening model fits.

The instrument is then just to bin the residual by the metal coordinate and plot the mean. Genuine beam hardening must produce a concave dip. On metal-heavy rays the measured attenuation falls below what the linear model expects. The plastic coordinate serves as a control. Nothing about ball positions, rows, or orientations enters the instrument.

The real scan's transfer curves
{{FIG:bh_transfer_real}}

How to read it: each point is the mean residual over rays binned by the metal coordinate m. The error bars are the per-bin standard deviation. That spread is mostly noise and unrelated structure. The mean is the systematic part. Left, padded: the mean dips on the metal-heaviest rays, −0.014 in the top bin against +0.005 mid-range. That is the hardening sign. The plastic-coordinate control (not shown) is flat. Right, unpadded: no metal organization at all. The residual rms is 0.113, against 0.060 padded. The truncation deficit therefore dominates everywhere. The residual's full organization by the metal coordinate is the hardening fingerprint. These results indicate that the fingerprint only becomes measurable once padding removes the larger unmodeled-mass source.

What the magnitude means — and does not mean. The residual is only the undeposited part of the hardening path deficit. A converged run with a weak prior absorbs most of the path deficit into the image. That absorption is the artifact. So the −0.014 dip is a lower bound on the injected nonlinearity, not its size. Severity calibration must therefore use the residual and the image together (§5). The synthetic sweep makes this concrete. There the residual dip is non-monotone in the true severity s. Stronger hardening deposits a larger fraction of the path deficit into the image.

3. The testbed: a parametric polychromatic forward model

The testbed aims for the correct structure of the nonlinearity with a single severity dial. It does not aim for spectroscopic fidelity. The testbed is the driver spectrum.py together with the materials mode of the ball-grid phantom. It has three parts:

  • Materials. The phantom decomposes exactly into a plastic slab plus per-metal ball maps. The ball maps hold multiple metal types with different hardening factors. The types alternate across the lattice in a checkerboard. The metal types also carry different reference-energy values. Here they are 6.0 and 4.5. The metals are therefore also separable by intensity segmentation.
  • Physics. The source is a filtered-bremsstrahlung spectrum. It applies Kramers' law × aluminum filtration over 24 energy bins. Each material's energy dependence takes the standard two-basis form: photoelectric ((E/Eref)−3) plus Klein–Nishina Compton. Each material also has one hardening factor wpe. The values are 0.15 for the slab, and 0.8 and 0.55 for the metals.
  • The severity dial. s ∈ [0, 1] interpolates each material's ratio curve toward flat. Unit testing prompted one refinement: each ratio curve is normalized so its spectrum-weighted mean is exactly 1. The linear model is then the tangent at zero path length at every severity s. Three properties follow from that: the dial bends pure curvature without recalibrating the small-path slope, the path deficit is nonnegative, and the path deficit is monotone in s. At s = 0 the model is bit-for-bit the existing monochromatic pipeline. Every run asserts that before any reconstruction.

4. Results: hardening alone produces the family, object-anchored

The attribution panels
{{FIG:bh_error_panels}}

How to read it: error against the reference-energy ground truth at iteration 16. The top row is the axial ball-layer slice and the bottom row is the (x, z) mid-plane. The window is ±0.006, or ≈ 0.6× the slab value. Left: no hardening, and the error is isotropic speckle. Middle: hardening as the only pathology. The slab region develops the slab-wide negative deficit (cupping). The darkening concentrates between and around the balls. In (x, z) the error develops z-localized bands at the slab faces. Right: the truncated-padded configuration with its denser ball grid. Grid-aligned interball dark bands appear in the axial plane, and face bands with fine vertical needle texture appear in (x, z). That is the character of the real padded scan's residual family.

Three checks pin the attribution:

  1. Object-anchored, not aliasing. The full-severity case was re-run with the view angles rotated by half an angular step. The error field barely changed. The correlation is 0.982 at the ball layer. Metal-pair hardening structure is fixed by the object. View-sampling artifacts would have rotated with the trajectory.
  2. The signed ledger. Deposited mass runs 0.000 → −0.079 → −0.170 across severities 0 → 0.5 → 1. These numbers come from the contained case. In that case the object is fully contained in the field of view. These results indicate that hardening removes mass. That is the mirror image of truncation's +32% deposit. One framework covers both families. Unmodeled mass of either sign is deposited at the expression scale the prior allows.
  3. Instrumental orthogonality. The vertical-family severity score Slow stays at the contained-null floor in every hardened case. The values run 6×10−6 → 1.3×10−5. That is measured confirmation of two things: the two families need separate instruments, and the Phase A instrument was never scoring this family.

The MAR coordinates check out against truth. On the synthetic testbed, transfer curves were binned two ways: by the true metal coordinate and by the MAR-segmented metal coordinate. The two agree within ~3% for the dominant metal. The class labels are ordered by intensity. On the weaker metal that ordering mixes somewhat. These results indicate that the instrument used on real data measures what it claims to.

5. The calibrated case

The severity sweep brackets the real padded scan's residual dip between s = 0 and s = 0.5. The estimate lands near s ≈ 0.2. The calibrated run uses that severity. It also uses a denser ball lattice, of smaller pitch and radius. The denser lattice gives many more ball pairs. That lattice is closer to the real package. The run covers both the contained and the truncated-padded configurations.

6. Where this leaves things

  • The real scan's error budget is now decomposed into two attributed families. Truncation gives the truncation family, the vertical, z-coherent deposit. That family has positive mass, and padding removes it. Hardening gives the hardening family, the ray-aligned interball bands. That family has negative mass, and padding leaves it untouched. Each family has a validated instrument: the two-seed / P(fz) pair for the first, and the residual transfer curve for the second.
  • The MAR work on the release goals list now has a testbed. The testbed supplies four things: ground truth, a severity dial calibrated to the real scan, multiple metals with distinct hardening factors, and the transfer curve as the acceptance instrument. A correction should flatten that curve. The natural first experiment is recon_plastic_metal on the calibrated case, scored before and after.
  • A user-facing demo ships alongside the library's MAR tools. The demo is demo/demo_10_mar.py. It runs a polychromatic ball-grid simulation. The data, hardening, reconstruction, and MAR parameters are set at the top of the file. It compares a standard reconstruction with a MAR reconstruction, and it shows the results in the slice viewer.
