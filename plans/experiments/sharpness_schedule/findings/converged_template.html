The streaks are in the solution: the converged reconstruction under truncation

The sharpness/snr_db schedule study, mechanism follow-up (2026-07-26), branch sharpness_schedule. Background: findings.html (the artifact and the instruments, Phase A) and phase_b_results.html (the schedule evaluation, Phase B) · scripts: plans/experiments/sharpness_schedule/mechanism/.

TL;DR

After the schedule search came back negative, the question turned to the artifact's late-iteration behavior. On the hardened synthetic testbed, the streak severity grows through the tail of the run. Is that slow, wandering convergence? Or is the optimizer heading exactly where the objective points? A two-start experiment settles it. A run started from the ground truth itself converges to the same streaked solution as the production run, while the objective descends cleanly in both. The streaks are not a convergence artifact here. They are the minimizer's own content.

  • Both starts merge onto one growing severity curve. The two curves agree within 8% by iteration 5 and are identical from iteration 20. Their final volumes agree to 1.6% max / 2% rms. The two starts reach the convex objective's single limit point from both sides.
  • Started at the truth, the optimizer walks away from it on purpose. Error against the ground truth rises 0.39 → 0.57 while the data residual falls 0.208 → 0.089. The streaked solution therefore fits the truncated data 2.3× better than the truth does.
  • The seed-dependent part of the artifact is a transient. It decays two decades over the long run. At the practical budget of 15 iterations it is still far from gone.
  • Where the error lives supports a simple mechanism. The reconstruction deposits +32% of the ground truth's mass inside the domain. That deposit is the out-of-view attenuation the reconstruction must explain somehow. Roughly half of it is packed into the boundary flash, the bright ring at the support edge. The other half is spread through the interior as fine-scale, z-organized texture, an interior version of the boundary flash.

Consequence: update-side remedies cannot help the persistent streak field. That field is the seed-independent part of the artifact. The fix must change the objective or the model. A same-day follow-up wave (§6) tested the mechanism and the leading remedy. The deposit's total is conserved across a 16× severity sweep, while its expression scale tracks the prior's strength. Lateral padding removes the artifact on the hardened testbed. Severity drops 156×. On the real scan padding removes the boundary flash and the entire late-iteration growth. That growth goes from ×10.5 to flat. The seed-dependent transient also decays faster. At the practical budget of 15 iterations, however, the transient itself drops only 23–25%. The streaks also form with no cone at all: parallel beam, truncated, at 134× the contained level. These results indicate that the mechanism is geometry-general. The cone acts mainly as the transient's amplifier. Direct numbers for the iteration-count decision follow, with a condition. More iterations pair with padding, not without it.

1. The question: slow convergence, or the destination itself?

Phase A established the artifact and the instruments. Phase B showed that scheduling the regularization by granularity makes the streaks worse, not better. That negative result pushed attention to a fact both testbeds kept showing.

One of those testbeds is the hardened synthetic testbed. It is the laterally truncated slab with the larger cone angle, described in Appendix B of the Phase A page. This page calls it the hardened testbed.

On the hardened testbed the fact is plain. The low-frequency-in-z streak severity does not just persist through the late iterations. It grows, slowly and steadily. Meanwhile every global convergence measure improves: the residual and the objective. The severity climbs monotonically from around iteration 2, with no sign of turning over.

Two very different explanations fit that observation, and they demand opposite remedies:

  1. Drift. The iteration dynamics keep injecting partition-shaped error into a subspace with almost no restoring force, so error accumulates orthogonally to the real descent. If so, shaping the updates could suppress the accumulation. One such shaping is damping the smooth-in-z component of each voxel-cylinder's update.
  2. Descent. Consider laterally truncated data at aggressive regularization settings. The objective then genuinely prefers a streaked solution. The run is converging correctly to a minimizer that contains the artifact. If so, no amount of update shaping can remove the persistent streak field. Only a change to the objective or the model can remove it. Three such changes exist: padding, weights, and prior structure.

Why it matters beyond remedies: the release plan contemplates raising the default iteration count from 15 to 25–50. Descent is item 2 in that list. Under Descent, more iterations mean more streak, not less. The artifact deepens as the run converges.

2. The experiment: two starts, one convex objective

The objective is convex. Its parameters are p = 2 and q = 1.2. Every start therefore converges to the same limit point. That turns the initialization into a probe. Run the same reconstruction from two extreme starting points, and watch which way each moves.

  • Production start: the standard initialization path. That path is a direct (FDK) reconstruction, scaled by the optimal scale α the code applies internally. This is the configuration every earlier run used.
  • Truth start: the ground truth phantom itself, cropped to the reconstruction cylinder. This start receives the same optimal scaling the FDK path receives. That scaling is α = argmina ‖y − a·Ax0‖2W. It is applied before the run. The scaling has to be applied explicitly, because an explicitly supplied start deliberately skips the internal scaling.

The protocol was fixed across both starts. Both starts ran for 60 iterations, four times the practical budget (15 iterations, so final index 14). Each start used two partition seeds, identical seed-for-seed, so any difference between them came from the start alone. Every iteration was scored on five tracks: the axial power spectrum P(fz) of the in-plane high-passed error, its low band as the streak severity Slow, the objective at the target regularization, the data residual, and the two-seed difference within each start. That last track measures the seed-dependent transient, the part of the artifact that depends on the partition seed. The geometry was the hardened testbed: truncated slab + 11.3° cone, ball layer at 0.35 of the height, default damping, sharpness 1.5 / snr_db 35.

The probe has two possible outcomes. Suppose the truth-started run stays near the truth while the production run's streak field keeps growing. Then the field is drift. Suppose instead that the truth-started run develops the same field the production run has. It moves away from the truth while its objective falls. Then the field belongs to the minimizer.

One number from the setup is telling on its own. For the truth start, α came out 1.267. The best data fit obtainable by scaling the true object therefore inflates it by 27%. The out-of-view part of the slab contributes attenuation that the in-view voxels must explain. Brightening everything is the first, crudest way the model can do that.

Four observations pin the verdict:

  1. The merge. Severity, its z-constant core, the residual, and the objective all become start-independent within a handful of iterations. The final volumes differ by 2% rms. That difference is residual start memory, still shrinking. With a convex objective this is exactly what "one minimizer" looks like.
  2. Clean descent. The objective decreases monotonically in every run. It agrees across starts at the end to five decimals. Nothing is oscillating, stalling, or diverging. The artifact grows along the descent.
  3. The walk away from truth. Started at the (scaled) truth, the interior error vs truth rises from 0.39 to 0.57 while the data residual falls from 0.208 to 0.089. The streaked solution fits the truncated data 2.3× better than the truth does. The objective is being improved by the artifact.
  4. The transient dies; the field remains. Within each start, the two-seed difference decays from ~10−4 to ~5×10−7 over 60 iterations. What persists is seed-independent: the solution's own content. At iteration 14, however, the transient is still two decades above its floor. Seed effects are far from gone at realistic iteration counts.

4. Where the error lives: an interior version of the flash

Why would the minimizer contain streaks at all? The working mechanism gives an answer. The streak field is the interior counterpart of the boundary flash. Greg proposed this mechanism on seeing these results. It is consistent with every measurement so far. The scan's rays pass through slab material outside the field of view. The measured attenuation must be explained by voxels inside it. The familiar symptom is the boundary flash, the deposit piled at the support edge. But the prior controls where such deposits can sit cheaply. The expectation was that a strong prior suppresses pixel-to-pixel variation and squeezes the deposit onto the boundary ring. §6a measured that split, and the measurement inverted the expectation. At aggressive settings the in-plane restoring force is weak, and depositing the out-of-view mass throughout the interior as fine-scale texture costs little. Fine-scale, column-coherent texture is exactly what the streak metric measures.

The converged error field, and the deposited-mass ledger
{{FIG:converged_error_panels}}

How to read it: error of the converged production run against the ground truth, on a ±0.03 window. That window is ≈ 3× the slab's value. The boundary flash saturates at this window, and the never-updated corners outside the reconstruction cylinder are blanked. Left: the axial mid-slice. Fine-grained, spatially disorganized texture fills the interior, with the saturated boundary flash at the support edge. Middle: the (x,z) mid-plane. The same texture is organized as vertical striping across the slab band. That striping is the artifact's defining z-coherence. The strongest deposits sit at the slab's top and bottom faces. Right: the mass ledger. Integrated over the reconstruction, the error adds +32% of the ground truth's total mass. Of that, +17% is packed into the outer 5% ring at roughly slab density, the boundary flash. The other +15% is spread through the interior at about a tenth of slab density. The interior deposit is fine-scale dominated. At the mid slice, the pixel-scale component carries about twice the power of the smooth component.

Three details tie the ledger to the mechanism. First, the deposit is positive on net in both zones. This is added attenuation, not symmetric noise. Second, the interior deposit concentrates at slab heights in z, and its per-slice mean peaks near the slab band. The deposit therefore inherits the out-of-view mass's own z-envelope. That envelope is a natural source for the field's z-organization. Third, the 27% over-brightening that α applies to the true object is the same phenomenon seen from the other end. The model wants more mass than the in-view truth contains.

Scope. These are hardened-testbed results. On the real scan the same fingerprints appear: the streak-band distance's late growth, its z-organization, the boundary flash, and the settings dependence. Even so, the closing experiments below are what check the transfer. One reinterpretation already firms up. The real-scan curves on the Phase A page measure the streak-band distance from the conservative reference, a run at sharpness 0 / snr_db 30. They are not measured against truth. Their late rise is error growing "away from the conservative reference". That rise is precisely what a run converging to the aggressive-settings minimizer should do relative to the conservative reference. It matches the truth-start curve here, with the conservative reference standing in for the truth.

5. Immediate implications

  • The remedy map splits by component. The persistent streak field is the minimizer's content. That field is the seed-independent part of the artifact. No update shaping, damping, or schedule can remove it. The fix must change what the objective asks for. Three candidates stand out, in order of current promise: lateral support padding, boundary/weight handling, and prior structure. Padding gives the out-of-view mass a legitimate home, and it is already the flash-remediation recommendation for this scan class. The seed-dependent transient is real at practical budgets, still two decades above floor at iteration 14. It remains a legitimate target for update-side tools. Per-cylinder smooth-in-z damping of the updates is the natural candidate, now aimed at the right component.
  • Iteration-count policy inherits a warning. On the hardened testbed, severity keeps growing as the run converges. Raising the default iteration count therefore buys convergence into the artifact. Whether the real scan shares this behavior, and how steeply, is measured in §6d. It does, and steeply.
  • The metric's meaning sharpens. Severity against a converged reference mixes "how far from converged" with "how streaked is the destination." The destination itself now carries the artifact. Two kinds of ruler therefore divide the labor cleanly: the reference-free two-seed difference, and the truth-based scores available on the hardened testbed.

6. The follow-up wave: the mechanism tested, the remedies measured

Three experiments ran immediately on these results. The first is the sharpness sweep of the deposit ledger (§6a). The second and third are lateral padding on the hardened testbed (§6b) and lateral padding on the real scan (§6c). The sweep is the mechanism's direct test. The two padding runs are the causal test, and padding is also the leading remedy. Two follow-ups came out of them: the real-scan long pair (§6d) and the parallel-beam probe (§6e). The long pair is the transfer test. The parallel-beam probe is the geometry test. All five completed, and each gets a verdict below.

The sweep, graphically
{{FIG:sweep_partition}}

Left: the total deposit (gray) is flat. It runs from 31.5% to 32.6% across a sweep that moves Slow 16×. The ring/interior split (orange/blue) moves only mildly. Right: what moves is the expression scale. The fine-scale fraction of the interior deposit climbs from 0.52 to 0.95 (green, left axis), and Slow (red dashed, log right axis) tracks it.

The hypothesis behind this sweep is that the streaks are an interior version of the boundary flash. That hypothesis made three predictions, and the sweep grades each one.

Conservation: confirmed. The total deposit is set by the data mismatch, and the prior cannot change it.

The ring/interior split: inverted. The prediction was that a stronger prior squeezes the deposit onto the ring. Measured, the ring share rises with sharpness and the interior share falls. A strong prior does not push the deposit outward. It spreads the deposit inward as a smooth interior bias, the "whole-interior bias" the flash pages characterized. A weak prior instead lets the deposit concentrate into a sharper ring spike and pixel-scale interior texture.

Texture: confirmed, and it is the part that matters. Severity tracks the fine-scale fraction, not the interior mass share.

The refined mechanism therefore has three parts. The data fixes how much mass must be deposited. The prior sets the spatial scale at which it is expressed. The fine-scale, z-organized expression is the streak artifact.

6b. Padding cures the hardened testbed — the causal chain closes

The hardened testbed was rerun on a laterally enlarged grid, at 1.5×, with the object fully inside the support.

Everything collapses at once. The deposit falls from +32% to +6% of true mass. The vs-truth Slow lands at 4.0×10−6 at iteration 59, 156× below the unpadded solution. The z-coherence ratio drops from ~88 to 1.2. That is, the artifact's defining axial organization is simply gone. Slow now decreases with iteration. The interior error vs truth falls from 0.57 unpadded to 0.195. It keeps improving. Give the out-of-view mass a legitimate home, and the interior flash does not form. The streaks do not form with it.

One detail is worth remembering. The padded solution slightly under-fills the original region, by −8% of true mass, so the exterior absorbs a little more than its true share.

6c. Padding on the real scan: the ring goes; the streaks mostly stay

{{FIG:pad_vs_unpad_bga}}

How to read it: iteration-16 finals, same seed, same settings. Left = unpadded baseline, middle = the padded run's central crop, right = their difference. The difference is dominated by a half-voxel grid-registration offset at the metal balls, because 1.5× of 510 gives an odd padding delta. Read the side-by-side panels, not the difference. What it shows: the padded run has no boundary flash arcs. In the bottom row, the baseline's flash arcs are gone. The padded run also reveals real board structure beyond the old field of view, at the right edge of the middle panels. That is exactly the flash-remediation result. But the fine vertical striping around the ball grid persists visibly. The two-seed Slow at iteration 14 drops only 23%, 0.00116 vs the baseline's 0.00151. That is far from the ≥2× a remedy would need.

The two-sided outcome is informative rather than disappointing. On the hardened testbed, lateral truncation is the only pathology, and removing it removes everything. On the real scan it is one contributor among several. The metal ball grid is still present, and with it beam hardening. Detector effects and possible axial truncation also remain. Absorbing the boundary flash therefore removes the ring but only a fraction of the streak field at the practical budget.

The discriminating follow-up is the padded long pair: 60 iterations at an even-delta scale of 1.502. That delta makes the crop voxel-exact against the conservative reference and the unpadded runs. That pair sharpens the padding verdict considerably.

Padding eliminates the late-time growth entirely. The unpadded streak-band distance grows ×10.5 from its minimum. The padded curve is flat to ±5% from iteration ~14 on. The z-constant component behaves likewise: unpadded ×12, padded flat. And the seed-dependent transient decays faster padded. Between conditions at iteration 59, the padded two-seed difference is 3× lower, 5.5×10−5 vs 1.7×10−4. That is consistent with the boundary flash no longer feeding partition-shaped kicks.

The flat padded curve sits at a higher level than the unpadded minimum. That level is measured against the unpadded conservative reference, which contains its own ring and deposit. The ruler cannot separate "the padded solution's residual streaks" from "the conservative reference's own artifact content". A padded conservative reference would split them, if ever needed. The growth verdict is ruler-robust either way. On the real scan, the converging-into-the-artifact dynamic is lateral-truncation-driven, and padding removes it.

6d. The real scan converges into the artifact too

It reads 0.012 at the minimum, 0.028 at iteration 24, 0.063 at 39, and 0.116 at 59. That is a factor ≈10, and the curve is still climbing. The two seeds are indistinguishable. Meanwhile the objective at target settings decreases strictly monotonically, and the unpadded finals agree to 2.3% rms (padded: 1.7%). These results indicate clean convergence to the aggressive-settings minimizer, with the growing streak field as seed-independent solution content. That is the hardened testbed's verdict, transferred. The growth itself is attributable to the lateral truncation.

The ruler carries one honest caveat. On the real scan there is no ground truth, and the streak-band distance from the conservative reference also contains legitimate sharp-vs-smooth solution differences. Genuinely sharper edges are in-plane-fine and z-coherent too. The ×10 growth number therefore overstates pure artifact growth by some amount. The direct check is to look at what the late iterations actually add:

The iteration-count decision now has real-data numbers — and a condition. On the default (unpadded) configuration, running past the practical budget moves the reconstruction monotonically deeper into the aggressive-settings solution. The streak-band distance from the conservative reference grows 2.3× by iteration 24 and ≈10× by iteration 59, while the data residual improves only from 0.1200 to 0.1133. Raising default max_iterations from 15 to 25–50 at these settings therefore buys little data fit. It also buys a steadily stronger expression of the deposit. With lateral padding the trade-off inverts. The streak-band distance is flat while the seed-dependent transient keeps dying within the run, 20× further from iteration 14 to 59. Extra iterations are then purely beneficial. Padding and a higher iteration count belong together. A higher count without padding makes truncated scans worse.

In parallel beam the projector couples nothing across z. Truncation alone still produces the z-organized streak deposit at 134× the contained level, and it grows through the run. The interior error vs truth is 0.575, essentially equal to the cone case's. The residual objective is 240× the contained case's. That residual is the deposit signature. The mechanism needs no cone. It is in-plane deposit physics plus the prior's z-coupling.

The geometry split also refines the cone's role. The cone is a moderate amplifier of the persistent streak field, ~3× here. It is by contrast a dramatic amplifier of the seed-dependent transient, 33×. In parallel beam a subset's kick cannot propagate across slices through the projector, so partition-shaped column-coherent injection is prior-mediated only. The shipped damping being cone-specific is consistent with where the transient actually lives.

6f. Where this leaves the remedy map

  • Lateral padding is the remedy for the growth. It removes the boundary flash. It also removes the late-iteration convergence-into-the-artifact dynamic. On the real scan that dynamic goes from ×10.5 to flat. Padding speeds the transient's decay, and it unlocks higher iteration counts. It does not reach the ≥2× two-seed bar at the 15-iteration budget. The two padded runs sit at −23% and −25%. The reason is that at short budgets the transient is partition dynamics, only weakly flash-fed.
  • The settings themselves are the other objective-side lever. Severity tracks the expression scale, so backing sharpness off directly coarsens the deposit's expression scale. That lever applies where a use case allows it. Mechanically, the artifact studied here is the price of aggressive settings under truncation. That holds in any geometry (§6e).
  • Update-side tools remain relevant only for the seed-dependent transient at practical budgets. That transient is still two decades above its floor at iteration 14 on the real scan. The transient is also cone-fed, 33× weaker in parallel. That fact explains the shipped damping's cone scope. It also keeps the per-cylinder smooth-in-z damping idea coherently aimed. The aim has two parts: same component, transpose direction. The idea stays parked pending a decision on whether the padded + more-iterations pairing already covers the practical need.
